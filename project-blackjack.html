Python Blackjack

A from-scratch terminal blackjack engine in Python — deck modeling, soft-Ace math, and dealer logic — reworked here into a playable browser demo.

← ALL PROJECTS

Concept & Rules

A single-player, single-round blackjack game built to run straight from the terminal. Standard rules: closest to 21 without going over wins, a two-card 21 is a blackjack, and Aces count as 11 unless that would bust the hand, in which case they drop to 1 one at a time.

Everything — the deck, the dealer, the hit/stand loop, and the payout logic — is built from plain Python data structures and control flow. No external libraries beyond the standard-library random module.

What It Does

♣

Card & Deck Modeling

A small Card class pairs a value and a suit; the full 52-card deck is built by crossing every value against every suit, then cloned per round so the source deck stays untouched.

♠

Soft-Ace Resolution

Every draw recounts the Aces in a hand and demotes them from 11 to 1, one at a time, until the total reads 21 or under — the same check runs for both the player and the dealer.

♥

Hit or Stand Loop

A simple input loop keeps drawing on "hit" and re-peeking the hand after every card, until the player stands or busts.

♦

Dealer Draws to 17

The dealer only plays after the player's turn ends, and always draws until its hand totals 17 or more — standard house rules, no peeking ahead.

♣

Full Round Resolution

Six explicit outcome branches: both bust (push), dealer bust, a tie, a dealer win, a player win, and a player blackjack paying out at a higher rate.

♠

Deck Exhaustion Guard

Both the player's and dealer's draw functions bail out safely if the shared deck ever runs dry mid-round, instead of throwing.

LIVE DEMO — RUNS IN YOUR BROWSER

A JS port of main.py's own deck, soft-Ace, and dealer-threshold logic — not a separate reimplementation. Adjust the bet, deal, and hit or stand until you either bust or stand on your total.

Payouts: a standard win pays 1:1, a two-card blackjack pays 3:2, a push returns your bet, and a bust (yours, with the dealer still in play) loses it. One quirk kept faithfully from the source: the dealer always plays its hand out to 17+, even in rounds you've already busted — exactly what roundFinish() does.

From Terminal to Browser

The original is a single self-contained round, run once from the command line, with placeholder payout text (+, winnings ,!) in place of real numbers — it was written to prove the rules logic, not to track a bankroll. The browser demo above loops rounds, reshuffles a fresh deck on every deal, and replaces that placeholder text with a real chip balance and real payout math, while keeping the exact same card values, soft-Ace counting, and dealer-stands-on-17 behavior as main.py.
