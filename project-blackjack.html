Python Blackjack

Concept & Rules

A single-player, single-round blackjack game built to run straight from the terminal, playing out a real house ruleset rather than just the bare hit/stand loop: dealer peek, insurance, natural blackjack, surrender, double down, and splitting (including the split-Aces exception) are all in play alongside the standard closest-to-21 rules and Ace math.

Everything — the deck, the dealer, every player decision, and the payout logic — is built from plain Python data structures and control flow. No external libraries beyond the standard-library random module.

What It Does

♣

Card & Deck Modeling

A small Card class pairs a value and a suit; the full 52-card deck is built by crossing every value against every suit, then cloned per round so the source deck stays untouched.

♠

Soft-Ace Resolution

Every hand recounts its Aces and demotes them from 11 to 1, one at a time, until the total reads 21 or under — the same check runs for the dealer and every player hand, including split hands.

♥

Insurance & Dealer Peek

An Ace up-card offers insurance for half the bet; an Ace or ten-value up-card triggers a secret peek at the hole card. A dealer blackjack resolves immediately — insurance pays 2:1, and the main bet pushes only if the player also has blackjack.

♦

Natural Blackjack

A two-card 21 (when the dealer doesn't also have one) pays out at 3:2 immediately, without ever entering the hit/stand loop.

♣

Double Down & Surrender

Both are first-action-only options on an unsplit two-card hand: double matches the bet for exactly one more card, surrender forfeits half the bet and ends the hand on the spot.

♠

Splitting, Including Aces

A matching pair can split into two independently played hands (doubling after split is allowed, re-splitting isn't). Split Aces are the exception: each gets exactly one card and stands automatically, with no blackjack bonus on a resulting 21.

♥

Dealer Draws to 17

The dealer only plays after every player hand is settled, and only draws further if at least one hand is still live to beat — a bust or surrender can't force the dealer to keep drawing.

♦

Deck Exhaustion Guard

Every draw bails out safely if the shared deck ever runs dry mid-round, instead of throwing.

LIVE DEMO — RUNS IN YOUR BROWSER

A JS port of main.py's own deck, soft-Ace, insurance/peek, and dealer-threshold logic — not a separate reimplementation. Adjust the bet and deal; hit, stand, double, split, or surrender as the hand allows.

Dealer shows an Ace or a ten-value card? The dealer secretly checks its own hole card for blackjack before you act, exactly like a real table — an Ace also offers insurance. A two-card 21 pays 3:2 immediately. Splitting a pair plays each half as its own hand (doubling after split is allowed, re-splitting isn't); split Aces get exactly one card each and stand automatically, with no blackjack bonus. Standard win pays 1:1, push returns your bet, surrender returns half. A bust is an unconditional loss the instant it happens — the dealer only draws its hand out if a live hand is still around to beat.

From Terminal to Browser

The original is a single self-contained round, run once from the command line — it was written to prove the rules logic, not to track a bankroll, so it used placeholder payout text instead of real numbers. The browser demo above loops rounds, reshuffles a fresh deck on every deal, and replaces that placeholder text with a real chip balance and real payout math, while keeping the exact same card values, soft-Ace counting, insurance/peek, split, and dealer-stands-on-17 behavior as main.py.
